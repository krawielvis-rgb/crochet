Crochet Sunglasses Case Tutorial

Small project, big joy! Learn how to crochet a cute, protective sunglasses case with a button closure — easy, beginner-friendly, and perfect for summer accessories.

Before You Begin

A crochet sunglasses case is one of the best first projects for this niche — it's a small rectangle, no shaping, no increasing, no complicated stitches. You chain the width of your glasses, work a tube tall enough to cover them, seam the bottom, add a flap, and sew a button. That's it.

The secret is the yarn choice. The sample in the Pin uses cotton yarn (worsted) and a 4mm hook. Cotton creates a dense, slightly stiff fabric that holds its shape and cushions glasses. Acrylic can be too soft and stretchy — your glasses would poke through.

Why this works for glasses protection: Single crochet worked with cotton + 4mm hook creates a thick, padded fabric (about 4mm thick) that absorbs light bumps. No need for fabric lining for everyday bag protection.

Materials & Tools

From your Pin:

  • Cotton yarn (worsted / medium weight): 40-60 yards. Choose smooth mercerized or unmercerized cotton. Beige, cream, natural like Pin is perfect for summer.
  • Crochet hook (4mm / G-6): Creates dense fabric that protects. If your fabric feels holey, go down to 3.5mm.
  • Yarn needle: For seaming bottom, weaving ends, sewing button securely.
  • Button closure (1 inch wooden button): One button + small elastic loop or chain loop. Wooden adds boutique look.
  • Optional: Stitch marker, scissors, tape measure, sunglasses to measure

Perfect For: Glasses protection, Quick gifts (1-2 hour make), Summer accessories, Beginners who want useful project

Sizing & Fit - Measuring Your Sunglasses

Don't use a fixed chain count — measure your actual glasses. Sunglasses vary wildly in width.

  1. Width: Lay glasses flat, measure across widest point (temple to temple). Your foundation chain should be this width + 0.5" for ease. Most adult sunglasses: 6-6.5" wide → chain 16-18 sts with cotton + 4mm hook.
  2. Height: Measure from top of frame to bottom of lens + 1" for flap overlap. Usually 3.5-4" tall body + 2" flap.
Fit checkpoint: Case should fit glasses snugly but not so tight you have to force them. Glasses should slide in without catching on stitches. If too tight, add 2 chains. If too loose, remove 2.

Abbreviations (US terms)

ch = chain, sc = single crochet, hdc = half double crochet, sl st = slip stitch, st(s) = stitch(es), BLO = back loop only, RS = right side, WS = wrong side

Complete Tutorial Roadmap

1. Chain width of glasses: Foundation chain matches glasses width + ease.
2. Work tube: Work flat rectangle in sc rows until height covers glasses.
3. Bottom seam: Fold and seam bottom closed securely.
4. Flap: Crochet flap extension over top opening.
5. Button closure: Add button + chain loop for secure closure.
6. Insert glasses: Test fit, weave ends, finish.

Step-by-Step Premium Pattern - Glasses Case

1Chain Width of Glasses

This foundation determines case width. Measure your glasses first, then chain.

Foundation (Adult sunglasses - Sample M):
Chain 18 (for 6" width with 4mm + worsted cotton).
Check: Hold chain against closed sunglasses. Chain should be slightly wider than glasses - about 0.5" extra on each side for seam and ease.
Tip: Keep chain relaxed, not tight. Tight chain makes case pucker at bottom.

If your sunglasses are oversized (7" wide), chain 20-22. For small reading glasses (5" wide), chain 14-16.

2Work Tube

Now build the main body - a simple rectangle that will become the tube protecting your glasses.

Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook, sc across. Ch 1, turn. [17 sts]
Row 2-28: Sc in each st across, ch 1, turn. [17 sts]
Length check: After row 28, rectangle should be about 7" tall (3.5" front + 3.5" back folded). It will fold in half to make 3.5" tall case.
Do not fasten off.

Why sc only? Single crochet creates the densest, most protective fabric. No holes for glasses to poke through.

3Bottom Seam

We now seam the bottom closed so glasses don't fall out. Two methods - choose one.

Method A - Fold and whip stitch (Beginner, recommended):
Fold rectangle in half with short ends meeting at top. With yarn needle and 12" tail, whip stitch bottom and side closed, working through both layers.
Method B - Sc seam (Neater):
Fold rectangle, sc through both layers across bottom and side. Creates visible ridge but very strong.
Secure: Weave tail through 4 sts forward, 3 back, trim.

After seaming, you have a pouch open at top, about 3.5" tall, 6" wide.

4Flap

Flap keeps glasses from sliding out and adds boutique finish with button.

Flap - Continue from body:
Row 29: Sc across top opening of pouch (17 sts), ch 1, turn. This is flap Row 1.
Rows 30-38: Sc across 17 sts, ch 1, turn. Flap about 2-2.5" tall.
Shaping (optional pretty curve):
Row 39: Sc2tog, sc 13, sc2tog, ch 1, turn. [15 sts]
Row 40: Sc2tog, sc 11, sc2tog, ch 1, turn. [13 sts]
Row 41: Sc across. Fasten off, leave 10" tail for button loop.

Flap can be straight rectangle for minimalist look, or slightly tapered like above for curved boutique look like Pin.

5Button Closure

Button closure is what makes this case functional - glasses stay secure in bag.

Button loop on flap:
Attach yarn at center of flap bottom edge. Ch 8-10 (test around your button), sl st back into same st to form loop. Fasten off, weave ends.
Sew button on body:
Position wooden button on front of case, centered, about 1" below top opening. With yarn needle, sew through button holes 4-5 times, going through case fabric each time. Pass through fabric to inside and weave tail through 5 sts in 2 directions for strength.
Loop size check: Loop should require slight stretch to go over button - snug, not loose.

Pro tip: Use same cotton yarn doubled for sewing button for extra strength. Glasses case gets opened/closed daily.

6Insert Glasses

Final test - your case is ready!

Final steps:
1. Weave in all ends: 4 sts one way, 3 back opposite way.
2. Turn case right side out if you seamed inside out.
3. Insert sunglasses - should slide in smoothly.
4. Close flap, loop over button.
5. Shake gently - glasses should not fall out.

If glasses catch on stitches, your tension may be too loose creating larger gaps. Go down to 3.5mm hook for next one.

Progress Checkpoints

  • Checkpoint 1 - Chain: Chain width = glasses width + 0.5". Relaxed, not tight curling.
  • Checkpoint 2 - Tube: Rectangle 7" tall, lies flat, edges straight, stitch count consistent 17 sts, dense fabric no holes.
  • Checkpoint 3 - Bottom seam: Bottom closed securely, no gaps, seam strong, pouch holds shape.
  • Checkpoint 4 - Flap: Flap centered, 2-2.5" tall, straight or gently tapered, same stitch count as body.
  • Checkpoint 5 - Button: Button sewn 4-5 times, loop snug requiring slight stretch, closure secure.
  • Checkpoint 6 - Finished: Glasses slide in/out smoothly, flap closes, case stands slightly, no loose ends.

Troubleshooting - Premium Fixes

Glasses too tight to insert

Chain too short. Frog and add 2 chains. Cotton doesn't stretch much - better slightly loose than too tight.

Glasses fall out, case too loose

Chain too long or hook too big creating loose fabric. Remove 2 chains or go down to 3.5mm hook.

Bottom seam has holes/gaps

Seam stitches too spaced. When whip stitching, go through every stitch, not every other. For extra security, sc seam instead.

Flap curls up

Tension tight or cotton twisting. Block lightly: dampen flap, pin flat, let dry. Or work flap in hdc for less curl.

Button loop too big, won't stay closed

Reduce chain loop to 6-7 chains. Should stretch slightly over button.

Button pops off in bag

Not sewn enough times. Sew 4-5 passes, then anchor yarn through case fabric in 2 directions. Double yarn for strength.

Fabric not protective, glasses poke through

Hook too big or yarn too thin. Must be worsted cotton + 4mm or smaller. DK yarn too thin for protection.

Finishing & Edging

Optional but gives boutique finish like Pin:

  • Edge case: After flap, sc evenly around entire case opening for crisp edge.
  • Weave ends: Always weave through 4 sts forward, 3 back opposite direction. Trim after secure.
  • Light steam block: If case curls, steam lightly and shape with hands. Don't press flat - you want 3D pouch shape.
  • Add stitch marker loop: Optional small chain loop inside to clip to bag with carabiner.

Variations & Custom Ideas

Different yarns

  • Beige/natural cotton like Pin - summer accessory bestseller
  • Cream + wooden button - minimalist Scandinavian
  • Pastel pink, sage, terracotta for seasonal gifts
  • Two-tone: Body in cream, flap + button in contrasting color

Size variations

  • Reading glasses case: Chain 14, rows 22 for smaller case
  • Oversized sunglasses: Chain 20-22, rows 30 for larger case
  • Phone sleeve: Same pattern but taller - 5" body instead of 3.5"

Add personalization

Embroider initial on flap with chain stitch, add small tassel to button, or crochet small flower on flap corner.

Matching set

Make matching earbud case (chain 10, rows 16) for gift set - sunglasses case + earbud pouch = perfect quick gift bundle.

Care & Storage

  • Cotton is washable - hand wash cold gentle, lay flat to dry. Keeps shape better than machine wash.
  • Remove glasses before washing case.
  • Reshape while damp, especially bottom seam and flap.
  • Check button stitching monthly if used daily in bag.
  • Store empty, flap closed, to keep shape. Don't fold flat long-term.
