Before You Begin

A crochet bookmark is one of those small projects that is perfect when you want to practice crochet without committing to a large project. It uses very little yarn, works up quickly, and gives you a useful finished item at the end.

This version is designed as a narrow rectangular bookmark worked back and forth in rows. The simple construction makes it especially suitable for beginners who are practicing chain stitches, single crochet, turning, and consistent tension.

Beginner tip: Keep your stitches relaxed. A bookmark should be flexible enough to slide between pages without becoming bulky.

Materials & Preparation

  • Lightweight cotton or smooth acrylic yarn
  • 3.0 mm crochet hook
  • Scissors
  • Tapestry or yarn needle
  • Optional: small amount of contrasting yarn for the tassel
  • Optional: measuring tape

Why lightweight yarn?

Cotton or another smooth lightweight yarn works particularly well because it creates a neat, relatively flat fabric. Cotton also holds its shape nicely and gives the bookmark enough structure to stay between the pages.

You can substitute another yarn weight, but remember that thicker yarn will create a wider and bulkier bookmark. If you change yarn weight, adjust your hook size so the fabric remains comfortable and flexible.

Gauge & Sizing

Gauge is not critical for this project because you can simply continue crocheting until the bookmark reaches your preferred length.

A useful starting size is approximately:

  • Width: about 4–5 cm
  • Length: about 15–20 cm

If you prefer a narrower bookmark, reduce the starting chain. For a longer bookmark, simply add more rows.

Size checkpoint

Place your work next to a book while crocheting. The finished bookmark can be slightly shorter than the book so that the tassel can extend beyond the pages.

Complete Tutorial Roadmap

The project is built in five simple stages:

  1. Create the foundation chain.
  2. Work rows of single crochet.
  3. Repeat the rows until the desired length is reached.
  4. Finish and weave in the yarn ends.
  5. Add an optional tassel.

Once you understand the basic repeat, you can make several bookmarks in different colors without changing the basic construction.

Step-by-Step Crochet Bookmark Pattern

Foundation: Chain 16.
Row 1: Skip the first chain from the hook. Work 1 single crochet into each remaining chain across. You should have 15 single crochet stitches.
Row 2: Chain 1 and turn. Work 1 single crochet into each stitch across.
Rows 3–30: Repeat Row 2 until the bookmark reaches your preferred length.
Final row: Chain 1 and work 1 single crochet into each stitch across.
Fasten off: Cut the yarn leaving a tail of approximately 10–15 cm. Pull the tail through the final loop and tighten gently.

Keeping the edges straight

The most common beginner problem with a narrow project is accidentally gaining or losing stitches at the ends of rows. Before turning each row, count your stitches.

You should consistently have 15 single crochet stitches across the bookmark.

Row checkpoint

After every few rows, quickly count the stitches. If you still have 15 stitches, your edges should remain straight.

Creating a Neat Border

A border is optional, but it can give the bookmark a more finished appearance. After completing the main body, attach yarn at one corner.

Step 1: Chain 1.
Step 2: Work single crochet evenly along the long side.
Step 3: Work 2–3 single crochet stitches into each corner so the fabric does not pull.
Step 4: Continue around the remaining sides.
Step 5: Slip stitch into the first stitch and fasten off.

The exact number of border stitches is less important than keeping the edge smooth. If the corners curl, add another stitch at the corner. If they become wavy, reduce the number of corner stitches.

Adding the Tassel

A tassel is completely optional, but it is an easy way to make the bookmark feel more decorative and gift-ready.

Step 1 — Prepare the yarn

Cut several strands of yarn approximately 15 cm long. You can use more strands for a fuller tassel or fewer strands for a delicate finish.

Step 2 — Create the loop

Fold the strands in half and use your crochet hook to pull the folded center through a stitch at the end of the bookmark.

Step 3 — Secure the tassel

Pull the loose ends through the folded loop and tighten the knot against the bookmark.

Step 4 — Trim

Trim the tassel ends so they are approximately the same length.

Design tip: Try using a contrasting color for the tassel. A cream bookmark with a dusty rose, green, or mustard tassel creates a simple handmade accent.

Important Checkpoints

Foundation

Your foundation chain should be loose enough to crochet into comfortably. If it feels tight, use a slightly larger hook just for the chain.

Stitch count

The main body should maintain 15 single crochet stitches per row.

Edges

Your sides should remain mostly straight. Small variations can usually be corrected by adjusting tension.

Length

Stop when the bookmark is comfortably shorter than your favorite book. Remember that the tassel adds extra length.

Troubleshooting

My bookmark is getting wider.

You are probably adding an extra stitch at the end of some rows. Count your stitches before turning. Every main row should contain 15 single crochet stitches.

My bookmark is getting narrower.

You may be accidentally skipping the final stitch. Pay particular attention to the last stitch of each row.

The bookmark is curling.

A small amount of curling can happen with crochet fabric. Try relaxing your tension and blocking the finished bookmark. A simple border can also help stabilize the edge.

The bookmark feels too thick.

Try a lighter yarn and smaller hook combination. Smooth cotton yarn is particularly useful when you want a thin bookmark that sits comfortably inside a book.

My tassel looks uneven.

Trim it gradually rather than cutting everything at once. Hold the strands together and make small cuts until the bottom edge looks even.

Finishing & Blocking

Weave the yarn tails into the back of the bookmark using a tapestry needle. Thread the yarn through several stitches in one direction, then change direction before trimming the excess.

For a particularly neat bookmark, lightly dampen the finished piece and lay it flat. Gently straighten the edges with your fingers and allow it to dry completely.

Blocking is especially useful if your edges have developed a slight curve or the stitches are uneven.

Final checkpoint

The bookmark should lie flat, have clean edges, and slide comfortably between the pages without adding noticeable bulk.

Variations & Gift Ideas

Once you've made the basic bookmark, there are plenty of ways to personalize it.

  • Use two colors for a simple striped design.
  • Make a rainbow bookmark with several short color changes.
  • Add a contrasting tassel.
  • Embroider a small initial onto the finished bookmark.
  • Use cotton scraps from other crochet projects.
  • Make several matching bookmarks for a book club.
  • Create handmade bookmarks as small gifts for teachers or friends.
  • Add a small crochet flower or heart to the end.

Because the project uses very little yarn, it is also a great way to use leftover yarn from larger crochet projects instead of letting small amounts go unused.

Care & Storage

Follow the care instructions for the yarn you selected. Cotton bookmarks can usually be gently washed, while acrylic yarn may be easier to spot clean.

Allow the bookmark to dry completely before placing it inside a book. Store it flat when not in use to help maintain its shape.

Final Notes

This crochet bookmark is a small project, but it is a useful way to practice consistent tension, straight edges, turning rows, and finishing techniques.

Once you are comfortable with the basic pattern, experiment with different yarn colors, stripes, borders, and tassels to create a collection of handmade bookmarks.

Most importantly, don't worry about making your first bookmark absolutely perfect. Small projects like this are one of the best ways to build confidence with your hook while creating something you can actually use.
