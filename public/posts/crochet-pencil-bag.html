Sunflower Crochet Pencil Bag

A compact everyday pouch built from sunflower granny-square panels. The finished bag is designed for pencils, crochet hooks, makeup brushes, or other small essentials, with a simple four-panel construction and a clean zipper finish.

SkillConfident beginner
Finished sizeAbout 10 × 20 cm / 4 × 8 in
Time2–3 hours

This pattern is written as a complete project rather than a loose collection of ideas. You will make four matching sunflower squares, join two squares for the front and two for the back, then join the panels around a zipper. Make one test square before starting the full bag so you can confirm your tension and finished dimensions.

Materials, gauge and finished size

Materials

  • Worsted / medium #4 yarn in four colors: brown center, golden petals, cream background and green border.
  • Approximately 20–30 g total yarn for four squares plus joining.
  • 4.0 mm crochet hook; use 4.5 mm if your tension is tight.
  • 20 cm / 8 in zipper.
  • Tapestry needle and scissors.
  • Optional stitch marker and sewing needle.

Gauge and size

Each completed square should measure approximately 10 cm / 4 in. Two squares joined side by side make a panel about 10 × 20 cm / 4 × 8 in.

Important: make one complete square before making the remaining three. If your square is too large, use a smaller hook; if it is too small, use a larger hook. Keep the same hook for all four squares.

Construction: the bag is made from four individual squares. Two squares form the front and two form the back. The zipper is attached to the two long top edges before the front and back panels are joined around the remaining three sides.

Complete sunflower square pattern

Make four identical squares. Keep your tension consistent so the four panels meet cleanly during assembly.

Round 1 — Brown center

12 dc
  1. With Color A, make a magic ring, or chain 4 and join with a slip stitch to form a ring.
  2. Chain 2, then work 12 dc into the ring.
  3. Pull the ring closed enough to remove the hole without puckering the stitches.
  4. Slip stitch to the first dc to close the round. Fasten off and weave in the tail.
Checkpoint: you should have a flat circle with 12 clearly visible double crochet stitches.

Round 2 — Six sunflower petals

6 petals
  1. Join Color B in any stitch.
  2. Slip stitch into the next stitch. In the following stitch work 2 dc, 1 hdc, 2 dc to form one petal.
  3. Slip stitch into the next stitch.
  4. Repeat the petal sequence around the circle. Each petal occupies one center stitch and is separated from the next petal by one slip stitch.
  5. After six petals, slip stitch to the first petal base. Fasten off and weave in the end.
Checkpoint: six petals should surround the brown center, with one center stitch between each petal.

Round 3 — Square base

4 corners
  1. Join Color C in a gap between two petals.
  2. In the first gap, work 3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc to create the first corner.
  3. In the next gap work 3 dc. Continue across the side gaps.
  4. At each of the remaining three evenly spaced corner gaps, work 3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc.
  5. Work 3 dc in every side gap between corners.
  6. Join with a slip stitch to the first corner group. Fasten off and weave in the end.
Checkpoint: the motif is now square, with four larger chain-2 corner openings and straight sides.

Round 4 — Green border

4 corners
  1. Join Color D in any corner chain-2 space.
  2. Work 3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc in the same corner space.
  3. Work 3 dc in each side space until the next corner.
  4. In the next corner, work 3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc.
  5. Repeat around the square, keeping every corner identical.
  6. Join with a slip stitch, fasten off, and weave in the end.
Final square checkpoint: four corners, matching side groups, and a finished size close to 10 cm / 4 in.

Join the four squares into bag panels

Do not assemble the bag until all four squares are finished, blocked if needed, and measured. Put the four pieces on a flat surface and choose the orientation of the sunflower centers before joining.

Front panel

  1. Place two squares side by side with their right sides facing up.
  2. Align the corner spaces and side edges carefully.
  3. Use a whip stitch or mattress-style seam through the outer loops of the matching edge stitches.
  4. Keep the seam tension even. Pulling too tightly will create a ridge or make the panel narrower than the squares.
  5. Weave in the joining tail on the wrong side.

Back panel

Repeat the same process with the remaining two squares. Compare the front and back panels before moving on: they should be the same width and height.

Panel checkpoint: each panel should measure approximately 10 × 20 cm / 4 × 8 in before the zipper and side seams are added.

Attach the zipper

A 20 cm / 8 in zipper is a practical match for this finished panel size. A slightly longer zipper can be trimmed only if the zipper construction allows it; otherwise choose the closest size that fits the top edge.

  1. Place the zipper face down against the top edge of the front panel. Center it so the zipper teeth run parallel to the edge.
  2. Clip or pin the zipper in place. Keep the sunflower panel flat; do not stretch the crochet fabric.
  3. Using a sewing needle and matching thread, sew the zipper tape to the crochet panel with small, even stitches.
  4. Repeat for the back panel, making sure the two panels face the same direction and the zipper pull moves freely.
  5. Open the zipper at least halfway before closing the remaining three sides. This is essential so you can turn the bag right side out.
Practical tip: if the crochet fabric feels too flexible for the zipper, place a narrow strip of matching fabric or ribbon behind the zipper tape as reinforcement.

Troubleshooting

The flower looks lopsided.

Check Round 2 first. There should be six petals and the same stitch sequence in every petal. If one petal is taller, undo that petal rather than trying to compensate in the next round.

The square will not lie flat.

Compare the number of corner groups and side groups. Extra stitches usually create waves; too few stitches or very tight corner chains can pull the square inward. Block a minor distortion, but correct a structural stitch-count error by undoing the affected round.

The four squares are different sizes.

Do not stretch the smaller square to fit the larger one. Recheck tension and hook size. For a neat pouch, all four squares should be made with the same hook and similar tension.

The zipper edge puckers.

The zipper tape may be pulling the crochet fabric. Remove the seam around the affected section and resew with the crochet held flat. A narrow backing strip can also stabilize the edge.

The finished bag is too narrow.

Measure the two-square panel before adding the zipper. If the individual squares are under 10 cm, the whole bag will be smaller. For a larger bag, increase the square size consistently rather than mixing different square dimensions.

Finishing, care and useful variations

Close the side and bottom seams

  1. With the zipper partly open, place the front and back panels together with right sides facing.
  2. Align the side and bottom edges.
  3. Whip stitch or mattress stitch the two side seams and the bottom seam.
  4. Work slowly across each corner so the green border remains aligned.
  5. Turn the pouch right side out through the open zipper and check every seam before trimming tails.

Finishing

Weave every yarn tail through several stitches on the wrong side, change direction once, and trim the excess. If the squares need shaping, lightly wet-block them before assembly and let them dry completely.

Care

Follow the yarn label for washing. For most acrylic yarns, gentle washing and air drying are safer than high heat. Empty the pouch before washing and reshape it while damp.

Variations

  • Classic sunflower: brown center, golden petals, cream background, green border.
  • Muted palette: rust center, mustard petals, oatmeal background and olive border.
  • Solid back: use two sunflower squares for the front and a simple two-square or crocheted panel for the back.
  • Hook case: make the finished panels longer and use a longer zipper for crochet hooks.
  • Makeup pouch: use cotton yarn for a firmer fabric and add a washable fabric lining.
  • Gift set: make matching mini pouches in different sunflower color combinations.
Pattern integrity tip: make the first complete pouch as your size sample. Record the square measurement, panel measurement, zipper length, and seam allowance before making more. Consistency matters more than forcing every project to an exact number.
