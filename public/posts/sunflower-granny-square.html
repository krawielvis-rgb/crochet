Sunflower Granny Square

A structured four-round motif with a dark center, sunflower petals and a clean square border. This is written as a repeatable pattern you can actually build into blankets, bags, cushions and panels.

Materials, gauge and finished size

Use four worsted-weight yarn colors for the clearest sunflower effect: Color A for the center, Color B for the petals, Color C for the background and Color D for the green border. A 4.0 mm hook gives a compact motif; use 4.5 mm if your tension is tight.

  • Worsted / medium #4 yarn: approximately 5–8 g per color per square.
  • 4.0 mm crochet hook; 4.5 mm is acceptable for a softer square.
  • Tapestry needle and scissors.
  • Optional stitch marker for the first stitch of each round.

Gauge target: after Round 4, aim for approximately 10 cm (4 in) square. Exact gauge is less important for a single decorative motif, but it becomes important when joining many squares into one blanket or garment.

Important sizing rule: make two complete squares before committing to a large project. If one square is noticeably larger, adjust the hook size rather than forcing the join.

The complete pattern

Read the pattern from the center outward. Every round ends with a slip stitch join. When the pattern says “corner,” it means the larger chain-space opening that will form one of the four corners of the finished square.

Round 1 — Brown center

  1. With Color A, make a magic ring, or chain 4 and join with a slip stitch to form a ring.
  2. Chain 2. Work 11 double crochet into the ring.
  3. Pull the ring closed firmly, but leave the center flexible enough that the stitches do not bunch.
  4. Slip stitch to the top of the first double crochet to close the round. Count: 12 dc.

Checkpoint: you should have a small, flat circle. If it cups upward, your tension is too tight or the center ring is too small.

Round 2 — Sunflower petals

  1. Join Color B in any stitch.
  2. Chain 3. In the same stitch work 1 dc, 1 hdc, then 1 dc. This creates the first raised petal.
  3. Skip the next stitch.
  4. In the following stitch work 1 dc, 1 hdc, 1 dc.
  5. Repeat steps 3–4 around the circle until you have 6 petals.
  6. Slip stitch to the first petal base. Fasten off Color B and weave in the end.

Checkpoint: six evenly spaced petals should surround the center. If you have five or seven, undo the round and check that exactly one center stitch was skipped between petal groups.

Round 3 — Turn the flower into a square

  1. Join Color C in a gap between two petals.
  2. Chain 3, then work 2 dc into the same gap. Chain 1.
  3. In the next gap work 3 dc, ch 1. Repeat around the flower.
  4. At four evenly spaced gaps, replace the normal ch-1 with a corner group: 3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc.
  5. Continue around until all four corners are formed.
  6. Join with a slip stitch. Fasten off and weave in the end.

Checkpoint: the flower should now sit inside a square. The four corner chain-2 spaces should be visibly larger than the side spaces.

Round 4 — Green border

  1. Join Color D in any corner chain-2 space.
  2. Work 3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc in the same corner space.
  3. In the next side space, work 3 dc.
  4. In the next corner, work 3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc.
  5. Repeat the side group and corner group around the square.
  6. Join with a slip stitch, fasten off and weave in the end securely on the wrong side.

Final checkpoint: each side should have the same number of three-dc groups, and every corner should have exactly one chain-2 opening.

Troubleshooting

The square is turning into a circle

The corners are not being emphasized enough. Check Round 3: four corner spaces need the larger 3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc construction. If the corners were placed randomly, undo back to the previous round and space them evenly.

The petals look uneven

Check that every petal uses the same stitch sequence and that you skip exactly one center stitch between petals. If the center is too loose, tighten the magic ring before starting the petal round.

The edges wave

Too many stitches or overly loose tension can make the border ripple. If the problem appears on every square, try a smaller hook. If it appears only on one square, compare the number of side groups before changing your hook size.

The corners are pulling inward

Do not pull the chain-2 corner spaces tight. They need enough length to let the square turn cleanly. Blocking can improve a small amount of distortion, but it should not be used to rescue a major stitch-count error.

Finishing, blocking and joining

Weave each yarn tail through several stitches on the wrong side, change direction once, and trim only after the tail is secure. Avoid tying a bulky knot directly behind a corner because it can show through when the square is joined.

For a sharper square, lightly wet-block the finished motif. Pin the four corners first, then adjust the side centers until the square is symmetrical. Let it dry completely before removing the pins.

For joining, make two squares first and test your chosen method. A slip-stitch join creates a visible ridge, a whip stitch gives a flatter seam, and a join-as-you-go method is useful when you already know the final layout.

Care and useful variations

For washable acrylic or superwash wool, follow the yarn label rather than assuming every fiber can be treated the same way. Avoid high heat unless the label explicitly allows it. Store completed squares flat so the corners do not curl before assembly.

  • Classic sunflower: brown center, golden petals, cream background and green border.
  • Muted palette: rust center, mustard petals, oatmeal background and olive border.
  • One-color version: use one yarn throughout and let the texture create the design.
  • Blanket scale: keep the same square size across the whole project; change color rather than hook size whenever possible.
  • Bag or cushion: make a test panel first and measure it before calculating the final number of squares.
Pattern integrity tip: if your first square measures 10 cm and your second measures 10.7 cm, do not average the two. Find the tension difference before making the rest of the project. Consistent squares make assembly dramatically easier.

What to make with it

Four squares make a small coaster-sized panel; more can become a cushion front, tote panel, scarf section or blanket. Before joining dozens of motifs, lay every square on a flat surface and rotate them until the flower centers and border colors create the layout you want.
