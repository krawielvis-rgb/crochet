The magic ring: a step-by-step tutorial

The small technique that makes amigurumi look professionally finished.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

The magic ring, sometimes called a magic circle, is how most amigurumi and anything worked in the round properly begins. It replaces the small, visible hole that a standard chain-and-join start leaves behind.

Why it matters

Starting a round with a chain 2, join with slip stitch leaves a permanent gap at the very center, which is fine for a granny square but very noticeable on a small toy's head or the base of a basket.

The basic steps

Wrap the yarn around two fingers to form a loop, leaving a tail. Insert your hook through the loop, pull up a loop of working yarn, then chain 1 to lock it. Work your first round of stitches directly into the loop, over both strands. When the round is done, pull the tail firmly to close the center completely.

It takes practice to feel natural

Most people fumble the first several attempts, the loop wants to fall apart before you get your stitches in. Practice it on its own a few times, separate from a real project, before you rely on it in a pattern.

A tight, closed center is the small detail that makes a handmade toy look store-bought.

If it still feels awkward, a chain 2 start is a perfectly acceptable substitute while you build the habit, just know the small hole will be there.
