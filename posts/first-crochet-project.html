Your first crochet project: a gentle place to begin

Forget the pressure to be perfect. A hook, a soft ball of yarn, and fifteen quiet minutes are more than enough to start.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 8, 2026

Every crocheter remembers the first time a little row of loops appeared beneath their hook. It can feel awkward at first—and then, wonderfully, it starts to make sense. Crochet is built one small decision at a time, which makes it a forgiving craft to learn.

Begin with a small, useful goal

Choose a project you can finish in an afternoon or two: a coaster, washcloth, or simple scarf. Big blankets are beautiful, but a quick finish builds confidence and teaches you more than a half-finished ambitious project ever will.

The only tools you need

  • A 5 mm (H-8) crochet hook
  • One ball of smooth, light-colored worsted-weight yarn
  • A pair of scissors and a tapestry needle

Light yarn lets you see every stitch. Avoid fuzzy fibers for your first project; they hide the loops you are learning to recognize.

Learn these three moves

Start with a slip knot, make a chain, then practice single crochet. That is enough to create a sturdy square. Keep your first rows loose and even; tension comes with repetition, not willpower.

Make the first version for learning, not for keeping.

Give yourself room to learn

If a row looks strange, pull it back and try again. This is called frogging, and experienced makers do it constantly. The yarn is patient. You can be, too.
