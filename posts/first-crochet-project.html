Your First Crochet Project

Start with a small square you can actually finish. This beginner-friendly project teaches the essential moves without overwhelming you.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 12, 2026

Your first project should be simple enough to repeat and useful enough to make you want another one. A small textured square is perfect: it teaches you how to hold the hook, control tension, turn rows and finish your work.

What you need

  • One smooth worsted-weight yarn
  • A 5 mm crochet hook
  • Scissors and a tapestry needle

Step 1: Make the foundation chain

Make a slip knot and place it on your hook. Chain 16 stitches, keeping every loop loose enough for the hook to move comfortably. This chain becomes the width of your square.

Step 2: Work the first row

Insert the hook into the second chain from the hook. Yarn over, pull up a loop, yarn over again and pull through both loops. That is one single crochet. Repeat across the chain.

Step 3: Build the rows

Chain one and turn. Make one single crochet in every stitch across. Repeat this row until the piece is roughly square. Count your stitches at the end of each row so the edges stay straight.

Step 4: Finish

Cut the yarn leaving a tail, pull it through the final loop, then weave the tail back and forth through the stitches with a tapestry needle. Trim the excess and gently flatten your finished square.

Your first win

Do not worry if your first square is uneven. The goal is to learn the movements. Make a second one immediately and you will already feel the difference.
