A no-fuss guide to choosing yarn

The yarn aisle is full of possibility. Here is how to pick a skein that makes your project—and your practice—feel good.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 8, 2026

Yarn labels can seem like a secret code, but they are simply a collection of helpful clues. The best place to begin is with your pattern: it will name a yarn weight and suggest a hook size.

Start with weight

Weight describes thickness, not how heavy a skein feels. DK and worsted weight are wonderful for beginners because stitches are visible and the fabric grows quickly. Match the number on the yarn label to the weight specified by your pattern.

Choose a friendly fiber

Cotton is crisp and great for bags and washcloths. Wool is warm, springy, and ideal for hats. Acrylic is affordable, washable, and available in almost every color. For a first project, a smooth acrylic or wool blend is a thoughtful choice.

Color is information

Solid, light-to-medium colors make it easy to find individual stitches. Save dramatic variegated yarns for later, when reading your work feels natural.

A yarn you want to touch is often the right yarn to start with.

Trust the label

The label tells you fiber content, length, care instructions, suggested hook size, and dye lot. If a project needs more than one skein, buy all of them from the same dye lot for consistent color.
