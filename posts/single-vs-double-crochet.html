Single crochet vs double crochet: what's the difference

Two of the most common stitches, and when to reach for each one.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

Single crochet and double crochet are the two stitches you will use more than any other, and the difference between them is really about height and density, not difficulty.

Single crochet: tight and dense

Single crochet (sc) is the shortest common stitch. Insert the hook, yarn over, pull up a loop, yarn over again, pull through both loops. The result is a tight, dense fabric with almost no gaps, which is exactly why it is the standard choice for amigurumi, where you do not want stuffing showing through.

Double crochet: taller and drapier

Double crochet (dc) adds one extra step: yarn over before you insert the hook, then pull through two loops at a time, twice. Each stitch is roughly twice the height of a single crochet, which means projects grow faster and the fabric has more give, better for blankets, garments, and anything that needs to drape.

Which one should you use

If the pattern calls for structure and durability, like a toy, a bag, or a coaster, single crochet is usually the better fit. If it calls for softness, movement, or speed, a scarf, a sweater, a lightweight blanket, double crochet tends to win.

Height changes everything: the same yarn, the same hook, a completely different fabric.

Most patterns will specify which one to use, but understanding why helps you make swaps confidently when you want to adapt a design.
