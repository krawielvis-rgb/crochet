How to Crochet a Scarf

Hey, it’s Sara — a soft ribbed scarf with a simple one-row repeat and clear sizing for adults and kids.

Sara Rain Crochet · Updated September 2026

I wanted this scarf to be the kind you reach for without thinking: soft, simple, a little oversized, and interesting enough that making sixty inches of it never feels like punishment.

ProjectSoft ribbed scarf
StitchBack-loop half double crochet
LevelBeginner

The scarf I make when I want quiet crochet

There are projects that need counting, markers, notes and complete silence. This is not one of them. This is the scarf I would choose for an evening on the sofa when I want my hands busy but my brain somewhere else.

The little trick is working into the back loop. It turns an ordinary half double crochet into soft ribs that fold beautifully around the neck. It looks more complicated than it is, which is always a nice bonus.

Yarn, hook and the feel I’m after

I like a worsted-weight yarn that feels good against the cheek. You will need roughly 350–400 g / 700–800 m for a generous adult scarf and a 5.5 mm hook. Acrylic is easy to live with; a wool blend gives you extra warmth and bounce. Before committing, crochet a few rows and actually rub the fabric against your neck. A beautiful yarn that makes you itch will become a beautiful scarf that lives in a drawer.

My target is about 7 in × 60–65 in (18 × 150–165 cm) before fringe, but scarves are wonderfully forgiving. Make yours for the person who will wear it.

The pattern

FoundationChain 26.

Row 1Half double crochet in the 3rd chain from the hook and in every chain across. You should have 24 hdc.

Row 2Chain 2, turn. Half double crochet in the back loop only of each stitch across.

Keep goingRepeat Row 2 until the scarf is the length you want.

Every ten rows or so, lay it flat and look at the edges. If the scarf is quietly drifting wider or narrower, count your stitches before going farther. I would much rather undo one row than discover the problem after an entire film.

How I decide when it is long enough

I stop measuring with a ruler eventually and wrap it around my neck. Can I loop it once and still have comfortable ends? Does it sit where I want it to? For a shorter scarf, around 50 inches can be enough. For the big wrapped-up winter feeling, keep going toward 70 inches.

For a child, I would make it narrower—around 4–5 inches—and roughly 40–50 inches long, then check the fit rather than treating those numbers as law.

The part that makes it feel finished

Weave the ends along the ribs instead of straight across them so they disappear into the texture. You can leave the scarf clean and simple, or add fringe: cut 10–12 inch strands, fold them in half, pull the folded loop through the short edge, then bring the tails through the loop.

I trim fringe only after the scarf has had a little shake and a rest. Freshly tied fringe never seems to agree on where the bottom is.

Why I like this one

A scarf is repetitive in the best way. Somewhere between the first awkward rows and the last one, you stop thinking about where the hook goes. You just crochet. And then you have something warm enough to wear out of the house—which still feels a little magical to me.
