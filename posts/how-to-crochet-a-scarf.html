Hey, it’s Sara

A scarf is still one of the kindest first wearables you can make. I’m Sara — this pattern is a simple rib (half double crochet in the back loop) so it looks polished without complicated stitch counting every row.

Materials & size

  • Yarn: Worsted (category 4), about 350–400 g / 700–800 m for an adult scarf. Soft acrylic or wool blend.
  • Hook: 5.5 mm (I-9), or size that gives a soft drape.
  • Scissors, tapestry needle.

Finished size (adult): about 7 in wide × 60–65 in long (18 × 150–165 cm) before fringe. Adjust width by chaining fewer/more stitches; adjust length by working more/fewer rows.

Ribbed scarf pattern (US terms)

Foundation Ch 26 (or any even number for your preferred width).

Row 1 Hdc in 3rd ch from hook and in each ch across. Turn. (24 hdc if you chained 26)

Row 2 and every row after Ch 2 (counts as hdc). Hdc in back loop only of each stitch across. Turn.

Repeat Row 2 until the scarf reaches about 60–65 in (150–165 cm), or the length you want.

Working in the back loop only creates that stretchy rib that looks store-bought.

Length check

Wrap the work around your neck the way you’ll wear it. Short scarves sit near 50 in; cozy wrap styles like 70 in+. Kids’ scarves: start around 4–5 in wide and 40–50 in long.

Fringe & finishing

Fasten off and weave in ends along the edge. For fringe: cut 10–12 in strands, fold in half, pull the loop through a stitch at the short end with your hook, then pull the tails through the loop. Trim even.

Wash note: follow the ball band. Many acrylics handle a gentle machine wash; wool usually prefers cool hand wash and flat dry.

Final note from Sara

If your edges wiggle, count stitches at the end of every few rows. Consistency beats speed. You’ve got a real wearable when you’re done — enjoy it.
