Crochet hook sizes explained: which one do you need

Millimeters, letters, and numbers, decoded so you can actually shop for a hook.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

Crochet hooks are labeled in three overlapping systems: millimeters, US letters, and sometimes numbers, and it trips up beginners constantly because a single hook can be called three different things depending on the brand.

The millimeter size is the one that matters most

The metric size, the actual diameter of the hook in millimeters, is the most reliable number because it is consistent across every brand and country. A 5.0 mm hook is a 5.0 mm hook no matter what letter is stamped next to it.

Letters are a US convention

US hooks are also labeled with letters, roughly from B (2.25mm) to S (19mm). The letters were assigned somewhat historically rather than logically, which is why they do not scale in an obvious pattern, always double check the millimeter size printed alongside the letter.

Match the hook to the yarn label

Yarn labels usually suggest a hook range for that weight. Worsted weight yarn typically pairs with a 5.0-5.5mm (H-I) hook, while fine fingering yarn wants something closer to 2.25-3.5mm. Amigurumi is the exception: many makers go one size smaller than recommended to keep stitches tight and stuffing hidden.

The right hook is less about the label and more about how your fabric feels once you're a few rows in.

When in doubt, swatch

If your stitches feel loose and floppy, size down. If your hand is cramping and the fabric feels stiff as cardboard, size up. A quick test swatch tells you more than any label ever will.
