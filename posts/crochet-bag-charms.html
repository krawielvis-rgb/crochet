Easy Crochet Bag Charms — 5 Mini Patterns for Beginners

Make a tiny handmade accessory for your bag, backpack, keys, or a gift. This tutorial includes a daisy, strawberry, cherry pair, sunflower, and heart using simple stitches and small amounts of yarn.

Sara Rain Crochet · Published September 13, 2026

What you will make

These five mini crochet charms are designed as quick projects. They are small enough for scrap yarn and can be attached to a swivel clasp, key ring, zipper pull, backpack, or bag handle.

  • Daisy flower charm
  • Strawberry charm
  • Cherry pair charm
  • Sunflower charm
  • Heart charm

Materials

  • Small amounts of worsted or DK yarn in the colors you want
  • A crochet hook that gives you a firm fabric; 3.5–4 mm is a useful starting point
  • Scissors
  • Yarn needle
  • Optional: swivel clasps, key rings, or lobster clasps
  • Optional: small amount of stuffing for the heart or strawberry

Abbreviations

US terms: ch = chain · sc = single crochet · hdc = half double crochet · dc = double crochet · sl st = slip stitch · inc = increase · dec = decrease · st = stitch · rnd = round.

Before you start

Keep your tension firm enough that the charm holds its shape, but do not pull the yarn so tightly that your hook cannot enter the stitches. Count each round before moving on. For a hanging charm, the attachment loop is just as important as the crochet itself, so secure it with several passes of yarn.

Daisy crochet bag charm

Colors: white petals and yellow center. Finished size is about 5–6 cm (2–2.5 in), depending on yarn and tension.

  1. Make a magic ring and work 10 sc into the ring. Pull the ring closed and join with a sl st. 10 sc.
  2. For each petal, work ch 3, 2 dc, ch 3, sl st into the next stitch. Repeat around for 5 petals.
  3. Fasten off and weave the ends through the back of the flower.
  4. Attach the hanging loop through the top of the flower, passing the yarn through several stitches before knotting and weaving the tail away.

Checkpoint: You should have five similar petals. If the flower looks uneven, check that each petal uses the same stitch sequence.

Strawberry crochet bag charm

Colors: red or pink for the berry and green for the leaves.

  1. Make a magic ring with 6 sc. 6 sc.
  2. Inc around. 12 sc.
  3. [Sc, inc] around. 18 sc.
  4. Sc around for 2 rounds. 18 sc each round.
  5. [Sc, dec] around. 12 sc.
  6. Add a small amount of stuffing. Dec around. 6 sc.
  7. Fasten off and close the opening. Add a small green leaf cap with a few sc stitches and secure it firmly.
  8. Add the hanging loop through the top, weaving it through multiple stitches.

Checkpoint: The berry should taper toward the bottom and remain firm enough that the filling cannot easily escape.

Cherry pair crochet bag charm

Make two small round berries using the same construction as the strawberry but stop before the final decrease so the cherries remain compact.

  1. Make a magic ring with 6 sc.
  2. Inc around to 12 sc.
  3. Sc around for 2 rounds.
  4. Dec around to 6 sc, add a tiny amount of stuffing, close, and weave the tail inside.
  5. Make two green stems with chains. Join the stems at the top and secure both cherries beneath them.
  6. Create the hanging loop from the joined stem area and reinforce the join before trimming the ends.

Sunflower crochet bag charm

Colors: yellow petals and brown center.

  1. Make a magic ring and work 10 sc. Join with a sl st.
  2. Work a petal around each stitch using ch 3, 2 dc, ch 3, sl st. Repeat around.
  3. Use brown yarn to embroider or crochet a small center over the middle of the flower.
  4. Secure the back carefully, then attach the hanging loop through several stitches.

Variation: Use a slightly larger hook and thicker yarn for a larger bag charm, or make the petals shorter for a compact keychain.

Heart crochet bag charm

  1. Make two small starting circles with 6 sc in a magic ring. These form the two rounded top sections.
  2. Join the circles by crocheting across both sections, keeping the center join tight.
  3. Work decrease rounds gradually toward the bottom point. Keep the stitch count symmetrical from left to right.
  4. Add a small amount of stuffing before the final decrease if you want a padded heart.
  5. Close the point, weave the tail securely, and add the hanging loop at the top center.

Checkpoint: The two top curves should be similar in size. If one side is larger, check the joining point and the number of decreases on each side.

How to make the hanging loop secure

  1. Choose the strongest point at the top of the charm.
  2. Pass a doubled strand through several nearby stitches.
  3. Attach your clasp or key ring.
  4. Wrap and weave the yarn through the same area several times.
  5. Pull gently to test the attachment before trimming the tail.

A charm gets pulled repeatedly when a bag moves, so avoid relying on a single knot or one stitch.

Troubleshooting

The charm is floppy

Try a smaller hook or firmer tension. A dense fabric usually holds a small charm better.

The shape is too large

Use thinner yarn and a smaller hook, or remove one increase round from the construction.

The hanging loop keeps coming loose

Reinforce the loop through several stitches rather than attaching it to one stitch. Weave the tail in multiple directions on the back.

The rounds look uneven

Use a stitch marker at the start of each round and count before beginning the next one.

Easy ways to customize your charms

  • Use leftover yarn from larger projects.
  • Make a matching set in one color palette.
  • Add a small clasp for a removable bag accessory.
  • Turn the daisy or sunflower into a zipper pull.
  • Make a strawberry or heart as a small handmade gift.
  • Combine several charms on one clasp for a colorful bag decoration.

Finishing and care

Weave every tail securely and trim only after the attachment has been tested. Clean the finished charm according to the yarn label. For charms that will be used daily, check the clasp and hanging loop occasionally for wear.

Keep making

These mini projects are useful for practicing small rounds, increases, decreases, color changes, and secure finishing without committing to a large project. If you are new to crochet, start with the first crochet project guide, then practice the magic ring before making the rounded charms.
