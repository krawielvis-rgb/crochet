Crochet vs Knitting

Both crafts turn yarn into fabric, but the tools and movements create different experiences.

Words by Crochet Journal · Updated September 12, 2026

The tools

Crochet uses one hook. Knitting normally uses two needles. That single-hook setup makes crochet especially convenient for carrying around and correcting individual stitches.

The fabric

Crochet tends to create a thicker, more structured fabric. Knitting often creates a stretchier, drapier fabric. Yarn and stitch choice can change both dramatically.

The learning curve

Many beginners find basic crochet quick to grasp because only a few loops are active at once. Knitting can feel different because several stitches stay on the needle.

Choose by project

Try crochet for amigurumi, granny squares, bags and textured blankets. Try knitting for socks, fitted sweaters and fabrics where stretch and drape are priorities.

Which should you learn?

If you already enjoy crochet, there is no reason not to explore knitting later. Skills transfer: yarn behavior, tension and pattern reading are useful in both.
