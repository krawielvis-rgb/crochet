Crochet vs knitting: which should you learn first

Both make beautiful things. They ask very different things of your hands.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

People often ask which craft is easier, and the honest answer is that they are easier in different ways, not that one is simply better.

Crochet forgives mistakes faster

In crochet, you are generally only working with one live stitch at a time, which means unraveling a mistake is usually a matter of pulling out your hook and picking up the last correct loop. Knitting has many live stitches on the needle at once, and a dropped stitch can unravel in a direction you did not intend.

Knitting produces a different drape

Knit fabric tends to be thinner, stretchier, and drapes more like traditional fabric, which is why sweaters are so often knit. Crochet fabric is generally thicker and more structured, which is why it excels at toys, bags, and textured blankets.

Crochet is usually faster to learn the basics

Most people can make a recognizable square within their first sitting with crochet. Knitting has a slightly longer runway before projects start looking intentional, mostly because managing two needles takes more coordination at first.

Neither is more advanced than the other, they are simply different tools for different fabric.

You do not have to choose just one

Many makers eventually learn both, using crochet for toys and textured pieces, knitting for wearables that need to stretch and drape. If you are picking a starting point, crochet's single-stitch forgiveness makes it a gentler first craft for most people.
