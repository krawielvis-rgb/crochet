How to join a new ball of yarn seamlessly

The moment every project eventually reaches, handled without a visible knot.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

Every project eventually runs out of yarn mid-row, and how you join the next ball affects whether that spot is invisible or an obvious lump in the fabric.

The standard method

Stop the old yarn a few inches before completing a stitch, then finish that stitch by pulling the new yarn through instead. This keeps the join tucked inside the stitch itself rather than sitting on the surface. Weave in both tails securely once the piece is finished.

Avoid tying a knot

It is tempting to just knot the old and new yarn together, but knots can work loose over time, show as a small bump, and are genuinely harder to hide than a clean woven-in tail.

The Russian join, for a truly invisible switch

For projects where a visible tail is unacceptable, thread each yarn tail back through itself with a yarn needle, effectively splicing the two ends together before you even reach that point in your work. It takes a bit more time but leaves no bump and no tails to weave in later.

A clean join disappears into the fabric; nobody should be able to find where one ball ended and the next began.

Plan the join at a low-visibility spot

When possible, time your yarn change to fall at the start or end of a row, or along a seam edge that will be less visible, rather than in the middle of a prominent flat section.
