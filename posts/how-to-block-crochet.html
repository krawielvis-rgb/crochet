How to block your crochet projects

The final step that turns a homemade look into a finished one.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

Blocking is the process of shaping and setting a finished piece with water or steam, and it is the step most beginners skip, even though it is often what separates a homemade look from a polished one.

What blocking actually does

It relaxes the fibers, evens out minor tension inconsistencies, opens up lace and textured stitch patterns, and helps a piece hold its intended shape and measurements.

Wet blocking, the most common method

Hand wash or soak the piece briefly in cool water, gently press out excess water without wringing, then lay it flat on a towel or blocking mat, pinning it to your target dimensions. Let it air dry completely before removing the pins.

Steam blocking for a faster option

Hover a steamer or iron on a steam setting just above the fabric, without pressing down directly, then shape the piece by hand while it is warm and damp. This works well for acrylic yarns that do not respond as dramatically to a full wet soak.

Blocking is patience made visible, a piece finally settling into its true shape.

Check the yarn fiber first

Natural fibers like wool and cotton block beautifully. Acrylic yarn blocks less dramatically and can be damaged by high heat, always check the yarn label before applying steam.
