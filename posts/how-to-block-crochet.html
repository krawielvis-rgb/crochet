How to Block Crochet

Blocking helps a finished piece settle into a cleaner shape and can make lace and textured stitches much easier to see.

Words by Crochet Journal · Updated September 12, 2026

Step 1: Check the fiber

Read the yarn label first. Wet blocking works well for many natural fibers, while acrylic often responds better to gentle steam. Do not apply heat blindly.

Step 2: Wet the piece

For wet blocking, soak the crochet according to the fiber's care instructions. Press out excess water without twisting the fabric.

Step 3: Shape it

Lay the piece on a blocking mat or clean towel. Smooth the edges into the measurements you want and pin the corners and curves gently.

Step 4: Let it dry

Leave the project undisturbed until completely dry. Remove the pins and check the shape. Repeat only if the fabric still needs adjustment.

Why block?

Blocking is especially useful for granny squares, lace, garments and pieces that need matching measurements before assembly.
