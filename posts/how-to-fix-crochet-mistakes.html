How to fix common crochet mistakes

Uneven edges, mystery holes, and stitch counts that don't add up. None of it is fatal.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

Every crocheter, no matter how experienced, ends up frogging a row now and then. The good news is that almost every common mistake has a simple, repeatable fix.

Your edges are getting wider or narrower

This usually means you are accidentally adding or skipping a stitch at the turn. Count your stitches at the end of every row for a while, and make sure you are consistently working into the correct first and last stitch, the turning chain trips up a lot of beginners here.

There's an unexpected hole in your fabric

A stray hole almost always means a missed stitch, most often the very first or very last one in a row. Slow down at the edges specifically, that is where the eye tends to skip.

Your tension is inconsistent

If some rows look tight and others loose, it is often about how you are holding the yarn, not your hook technique. Try wrapping the working yarn around your pinky or through your fingers the same way every single row, consistency in your grip translates directly into consistency in the fabric.

Frogging is not failure, it is just crochet's version of pressing undo.

When to rip back versus when to live with it

A small, single mistake several rows back, in a project that will be gifted or used casually, is often not worth ripping out for. Save the unraveling for mistakes that actually affect the structure or size of the piece.
