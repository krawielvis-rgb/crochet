Getting your crochet tension right

Too tight, too loose, and how to find the middle that actually works.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

Tension is simply how tightly or loosely you pull the yarn as you work, and it affects everything from how a fabric drapes to whether your finished piece matches the pattern's intended size.

How to tell if your tension is too tight

If your hand cramps quickly, if you have to force the hook through each stitch, or if your fabric feels stiff and board-like, your tension is likely too tight. This often comes from gripping the working yarn too firmly.

How to tell if it's too loose

Floppy fabric, stitches that look uneven in size, or a piece that comes out noticeably larger than the pattern's stated gauge usually point to loose tension, often from letting too much slack build up before each stitch.

Consistency matters more than tightness or looseness

A slightly tight or slightly loose tension is not actually a problem as long as it is consistent throughout the piece. What creates visible unevenness is tension that shifts row to row or stitch to stitch.

Even tension is a habit in your hands, not a rule you have to think about every stitch.

Practice with a gauge swatch

Before starting a garment where size matters, crochet a small test square and measure it against the pattern's stated gauge. If it does not match, adjust your hook size up or down rather than trying to consciously change your grip mid-project.
