How to crochet simple coasters

A tiny, forgiving project that uses almost no yarn and teaches a lot.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

Coasters might be the single best first project in all of crochet. They use very little yarn, finish in well under an hour, and are genuinely useful the moment they are done.

The simplest approach: a flat circle or square

A magic ring with 6 single crochet increasing each round to about 4 inches across makes a tidy round coaster. A simple granny square, worked to the same size, makes an equally good square version.

Use a sturdier stitch than you would for a blanket

Single crochet or half double crochet, worked in a slightly firmer cotton yarn, holds up much better under a warm mug than a loose, drapey double crochet would.

Cotton is worth the extra cost here

Coasters get wet, get set down on tables, and get washed. Cotton yarn handles moisture and washing far better than acrylic, and it is a small enough project that the extra cost barely matters.

A coaster is a full, finished object, and it only takes an evening.

A batch makes a genuinely nice gift

A set of four or six coasters, in coordinating or varied colors, is a fast, low-pressure gift that still feels intentional and handmade.
