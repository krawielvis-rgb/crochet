Best yarn for amigurumi projects

Not all yarn behaves the same once it's stuffed and squeezed by small hands.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

Amigurumi puts different demands on yarn than a blanket or scarf does. It gets stuffed tight, squeezed, washed, and often handled by small children, so a few properties matter more here than elsewhere.

Cotton and cotton blends are the classic choice

100% cotton or a cotton-acrylic blend holds its shape well and has enough structure to keep a tight stitch from stretching out of place under stuffing pressure. It is also easy to wash, which matters for toys that will actually get used.

Avoid fuzzy or mohair-type yarns for your first toys

Fuzzy fibers hide your stitch definition, which makes it much harder to see where increases and decreases happen. Save the fluffy yarns for after you are comfortable with amigurumi shaping.

Worsted weight is the most forgiving starting point

It is thick enough to work up at a reasonable pace, and thin enough to keep small features like ears and paws from looking bulky. Fingering weight makes exquisite miniature toys but demands much more patience and precision.

The right yarn makes tight, tidy stitches feel almost automatic.

Color choice affects visible mistakes

Light, solid colors show every stitch clearly, which helps while you are learning shaping, but also shows every small inconsistency. Variegated or darker yarns hide minor unevenness if you would rather not stress about perfection on your first few toys.
