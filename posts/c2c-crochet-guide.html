Corner to corner (C2C) crochet for beginners

A blanket technique that builds diagonally, one square block at a time.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

Corner to corner, usually shortened to C2C, builds a piece diagonally out of small square blocks rather than working straight across in rows, and it has become especially popular for pixel-style graphghan blankets.

The basic structure

Each block is typically three double crochet stitches worked into a small chain space, and you build the fabric by adding blocks at the end of each row and, once you reach the widest point, decreasing blocks at the start of each row to taper back down to a point.

Why it suits graphghans and pixel designs

Because each block is a distinct unit, C2C is easy to color-change block by block, which makes it a natural fit for following a pixel chart to create a picture or pattern across a blanket.

It takes a little getting used to

The diagonal growth and the increase-then-decrease structure feels different from straight rows at first. Many makers find a written pattern or a simple graphghan chart much easier to follow for their first C2C project than trying to freestyle it.

Block by block, a diagonal grid slowly becomes a picture.

Start small

A coaster or small square practice piece is a much better first C2C project than diving straight into a full blanket, it lets you get comfortable with the increase and decrease rhythm before committing to something large.
