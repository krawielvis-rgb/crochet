How to crochet your first blanket

A forgiving project that teaches patience, one row at a time.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

A blanket sounds like an intimidating first project, but it is actually one of the most forgiving. There is no fitted shape to get wrong, just rows, and the size is entirely up to you.

Pick a simple, repetitive stitch

Single crochet or half double crochet in straight rows is the easiest place to start. Save textured or shell stitches for your second blanket, once your tension is more consistent.

Decide on size before you start, not after

A lapghan (about 36 x 48 inches) is a realistic first-blanket size, big enough to feel like a real project, small enough to actually finish. A full bed blanket is a wonderful second or third project once you know your pace.

Choose yarn that forgives uneven tension

A worsted weight acrylic or acrylic blend in a solid or subtly variegated color hides minor tension inconsistencies far better than a fine, smooth, solid-color yarn does.

A blanket is just one row, repeated until it becomes something warm.

Break it into sessions, not one sitting

Plan on working a set number of rows per week rather than trying to finish quickly. Blankets are a marathon project, and pacing yourself is what actually gets them across the finish line.
