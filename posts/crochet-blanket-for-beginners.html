BEGINNER BLANKET · EASY PATTERN

Crochet Blanket for Beginners

A simple lap blanket built from repeating half-double crochet rows so you can focus on even edges.

Crochet Journal · Updated September 12, 2026

A blanket is not difficult because the stitches are complicated. It is difficult because it asks you to come back, row after row, until a small beginning becomes something big enough to curl up under.

ProjectEveryday crochet blanket
StitchHalf double crochet
LevelPatient beginner

The blanket I would choose for a first one

For a first blanket, I do not want twelve stitch changes and a border that needs its own diagram. I want a yarn I enjoy touching, one stitch I can recognize, and rows long enough to become relaxing.

This pattern is deliberately plain. That gives color and texture room to do the pretty work. It also means you can watch the fabric grow without stopping every five minutes to decode the next instruction.

Before you make eighty chains and change your mind

Use worsted-weight yarn and a 5.5 mm hook. For a blanket around 36 × 48 in (91 × 122 cm), plan on roughly 1,200–1,500 yd / 1,100–1,370 m, depending on the yarn and your tension. Buy enough at once if you can, especially if dye lots are listed.

Make a small swatch first. Not because I enjoy assigning homework, but because five minutes with a swatch tells you whether the fabric is soft enough. If it feels like a placemat, go up a hook size.

The simple pattern

  1. Foundation: Chain 82.
  2. Row 1: Half double crochet in the 3rd chain from the hook and across. 80 hdc.
  3. Rows 2–64: Chain 2, turn, and half double crochet in every stitch across. Keep 80 stitches.
  4. Border: Chain 1 and single crochet evenly around the blanket, working 3 single crochets into each corner. Join with a slip stitch.
  5. Finish: Weave the ends in securely and wash according to the yarn label.

If you want stripes, change color every eight rows. Eight-row stripes are wide enough to feel intentional without leaving you with a hundred ends to hide later.

The middle-of-the-blanket problem

The beginning is exciting. The last ten rows are exciting. Somewhere in the middle, a blanket can feel like it has stopped growing completely. This is where I stop thinking about finishing it. I tell myself I am doing four rows tonight. Four rows is manageable. Eventually all those ordinary evenings become a blanket.

I also count every few rows instead of every row. If the sides are straight and I still have 80 stitches, I leave myself alone and enjoy the repetition.

If the edges start wandering

A narrowing blanket usually means the last stitch is being missed. A widening one usually means an extra stitch has slipped in near the turning chain. Put a marker in the first and last stitch for a few rows until your eye learns to spot them.

If the foundation edge feels tighter than the rest, that is common. On the next blanket, make the starting chain with a hook half a size larger, then switch back for Row 1.

The border is the little frame

I like a simple single-crochet border because it tidies the edges without stealing attention from the blanket. Do not force a fixed number of stitches down the row ends. Space them evenly and keep checking that the fabric lies flat. If the edge ruffles, there are too many; if it pulls inward, there are too few.

The best part

When I finish a blanket, I always notice how small that first chain seems compared with what it became. That is probably why blankets feel special. No single row looks impressive. The finished thing is just all of those quiet rows kept together.
