How to Read Crochet Patterns

Turn a page of abbreviations and punctuation into a clear sequence of stitches.

Words by Crochet Journal · Updated September 12, 2026

Step 1: Read the notes first

Before starting, check the pattern's yarn, hook, gauge, terminology and special instructions. Patterns may use US or UK stitch names, so confirm which system is used.

Step 2: Learn the abbreviations

Common US abbreviations include sc for single crochet, hdc for half double crochet, dc for double crochet and sl st for slip stitch. Keep the pattern's abbreviation key nearby.

Step 3: Understand repeats

Words inside brackets, parentheses or asterisks often tell you to repeat a group. If a line says “*dc, ch 1; repeat from *,” return to the asterisk after completing the group.

Step 4: Use stitch counts

Counts at the end of rows are checkpoints. If the pattern says you should have 24 stitches and you have 23, stop and find the missing stitch before continuing.

Read slowly

Mark your current row with a pencil or removable clip. Patterns become much easier when you treat each line as one small instruction rather than one giant task.
