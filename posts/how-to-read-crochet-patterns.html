Hey, it’s Sara

Reading a pattern is a skill of its own — separate from making the stitches. I’m Sara, and the day patterns stopped feeling like riddles was the day I started reading them like cooking recipes: ingredients first, then method, one line at a time.

Anatomy of a pattern

  1. Title & level — beginner / easy / intermediate.
  2. Materials — yarn weight, approximate yardage, hook, notions.
  3. Gauge — stitches and rows over 4 in / 10 cm. Matters for clothes; less critical for scarves and toys if you’re flexible on size.
  4. Abbreviations — always check; designers vary.
  5. Notes — turned rows vs rounds, special stitches, construction order.
  6. Instructions — the actual rows/rounds.

Repeats, brackets, asterisks

  • *…* repeat — do the bit between asterisks as many times as written.
  • ( … ) in next st / space — all stitches inside the parentheses go into the same place.
  • [ … ] 3 times — do the whole bracketed sequence three times.

A tiny pattern to decode

Ch 11.
Row 1: Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. Turn. (10)
Row 2: Ch 1, sc in each st across. Turn. (10)
Repeat Row 2 until piece measures 4 in (10 cm). Fasten off.

Translation: make a small rectangle 10 stitches wide and 4 inches tall in single crochet. The number in parentheses is your checkpoint count at the end of the row.

Habits that save frogging

  • Read the entire pattern once before you start — especially the finishing section.
  • Highlight the row you’re on.
  • Count stitches at the end of every row for the first ten rows until the rhythm settles.
  • When something feels wrong, stop at the end of the row. Don’t “hope it blocks out” for ten more rows.
Sara’s permission slip: frogging is not failure. It’s editing. Every maker does it.

Final note from Sara

Patterns are maps, not tests. Go slow, count often, and ask for help when a line doesn’t make sense — including from future-you after a cup of tea.
