How to read a crochet pattern without feeling lost

Patterns look like a foreign language at first. Here's how to actually decode one.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

The first time you open a real crochet pattern, it can look like code. Rows of abbreviations, asterisks, brackets, numbers in parentheses. It is not actually complicated, it is just a shorthand, and once you know the shorthand it reads as easily as a recipe.

Start with the abbreviations key

Every pattern includes a list of abbreviations near the top, usually things like ch (chain), sc (single crochet), dc (double crochet), st (stitch), and rep (repeat). Read this list first, even if you think you know it. Some designers use regional variations, and US versus UK terms swap the meaning of single and double crochet entirely.

Asterisks and brackets mean repeat

A line like *sc in next 2 sts, dc in next st* repeat from * to end simply means: do the instructions between the asterisks over and over until the row is finished. Brackets work the same way, often with a number after them, like [sc, dc] twice, which just means do that sequence exactly twice.

Numbers in parentheses are your stitch count

At the end of a row you will often see a number in parentheses, like (24 sts). This tells you how many stitches you should have when the row is done. It is the single best way to catch a mistake early, count your stitches at the end of every row until it becomes automatic.

A pattern is just someone else's notes, written so you can follow along at your own pace.

Read the whole pattern before you start

Skim the entire pattern before picking up your hook. Note the finished size, the yarn weight, and whether there are any techniques you have not tried yet. Five minutes of reading can save an hour of frogging.
