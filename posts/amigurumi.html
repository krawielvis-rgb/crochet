Hey, it’s Sara

Amigurumi looks intimidating until you realize most “creatures” are just spheres, tubes, and a bit of sewing. I’m Sara — and the tiny magic of amigurumi is that one weekend project can become a gift, a desk buddy, or the thing that finally made you fall in love with working in the round.

Materials for a first friend

  • Yarn: DK or worsted, smooth (not fuzzy) so you can see stitches. Cotton or acrylic.
  • Hook: 1–2 sizes smaller than the label suggests so the fabric is dense (e.g. worsted with 3.5–4.0 mm).
  • Polyester stuffing, 6–9 mm safety eyes (or embroidered eyes for littles), stitch marker, tapestry needle, scissors.

The three skills you need

  1. Magic ring — closed center for heads and bodies. (See my magic ring tutorial if this is new.)
  2. Single crochet in the round — continuous spiral; mark the first stitch of every round.
  3. Increases & decreases — 2 sc in one stitch to grow; sc2tog to shrink.

Practice: a simple sphere (US terms)

This is the head/body shape under almost every amigurumi.

Rounds R1: 6 sc in magic ring (6)
R2: 2 sc in each st (12)
R3: *sc, 2 sc in next* repeat (18)
R4: *sc 2, 2 sc in next* repeat (24)
R5–R8: sc around (24) — add or remove plain rounds for a taller/shorter shape
R9: *sc 2, sc2tog* repeat (18)
R10: *sc, sc2tog* repeat (12)
Stuff firmly.
R11: sc2tog around (6). Fasten off, weave through front loops, pull closed.

Once that sphere feels automatic, add ears (small half-circles), a snout (short tube), or limbs (thin tubes of sc).

Stuffing, eyes, and finishing

  • Stuff a little at a time — overstuffed seams split; understuffed toys look sad.
  • Place safety eyes before you close the head. Symmetry > perfection.
  • For babies and pets, embroider eyes instead of using plastic safety eyes.
  • Weave ends inside the body where they won’t show or poke.
Sara’s encouragement: your first animal may look slightly deranged. Mine did. The fifth one will make you grin. Keep going.

Final note from Sara

Amigurumi is patience practice disguised as cute. One round at a time.
