The tiny magic of amigurumi

A few rounds, a little stuffing, and suddenly there is a small creature keeping you company.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 8, 2026

Amigurumi is the Japanese craft of crocheted, stuffed toys. It is tiny in scale but rich in personality: an ideal project when you want a finish line by Sunday evening.

The stitch that does the work

Most amigurumi is worked in continuous rounds of single crochet. A magic ring creates the neat, closed centre. From there, increases make a shape wider and decreases bring it back in.

Materials matter

Choose cotton or a cotton blend with a smaller hook than the label suggests. The slightly tight tension keeps the stuffing inside. You will also need polyester filling, a stitch marker, and safety eyes or embroidery thread.

Let it be a little imperfect

The tiny tilt of an ear or a slightly uneven smile is often what gives a handmade toy its character. Aim for sturdy stitches, then let the personality arrive.

Small projects are not small achievements.

A gentle first project

A round jellyfish, a simple whale, or a tiny mushroom is a brilliant introduction. Pick a pattern with one main shape and only a few sewn details, then make a second one in a different color.
