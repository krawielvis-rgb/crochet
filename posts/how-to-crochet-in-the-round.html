How to crochet in the round

The technique behind hats, amigurumi, and anything with no straight edges.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 11, 2026

Working in the round is how hats, amigurumi, baskets, and doilies all take shape, and it is a different rhythm than working back and forth in rows, but not a harder one.

Two main approaches: joined rounds and spirals

A joined round means you complete a full circle of stitches, then join to the first stitch with a slip stitch before starting the next round, this creates a visible seam but keeps rounds clearly separated. A continuous spiral skips the join entirely, which is standard for amigurumi and gives a seamless look, but means you need a stitch marker to track where each round begins.

Use a stitch marker, always

Whether joining or spiraling, mark the first stitch of each round. Without a marker it is very easy to lose track of where a round starts, especially in continuous spiral work where there is no join to signal it visually.

Increases control the shape

A flat circle needs a specific increase pattern, typically adding 6 stitches per round, to stay flat rather than cupping or rippling. A pattern will specify exactly where to increase, follow it precisely on your first few attempts before experimenting.

A circle that lies flat is just consistent math, worked one stitch at a time.

If your circle starts to ruffle at the edges, you are increasing too often. If it starts to cup like a bowl, you are not increasing enough.
