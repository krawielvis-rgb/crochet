Hey, it’s Sara

If you’re new here — welcome. I’m Sara, and the classic granny square is the first motif I ever fell in love with. One small square, a few color changes, and suddenly you’ve got coasters, bags, blankets, cardigans… the whole world.

This is a real, countable pattern — not vague “make a square” advice. We’ll work in joined rounds, use US terms, and I’ll tell you the stitch counts so you can check yourself after every round.

Materials

Yarn & hook

  • Yarn: Worsted weight (category 4), about 15–20 g per square in the main color. Cotton or acrylic both work.
  • Hook: 5.0 mm (H-8). Go up to 5.5 mm if your tension runs tight.
  • Scissors, tapestry needle, stitch marker (optional).

Finished size

With worsted + 5.0 mm, one square after 4 rounds is about 4.5–5 in (11–13 cm) across. Add rounds for a bigger motif; keep the same corner math every time.

Abbreviations (US terms)

ch chain · sl st slip stitch · dc double crochet · sp space · st(s) stitch(es)

Classic granny square pattern

Start with a magic ring, or ch 4 and join with a sl st to form a ring — either is fine.

Round 1 Ch 3 (counts as first dc). Work 2 dc into the ring, ch 2. *3 dc into the ring, ch 2; repeat from * 3 more times. Join with sl st to top of beginning ch-3. You should have 4 groups of 3 dc and 4 corner ch-2 spaces.
Round 2 Sl st into the next ch-2 corner space. Ch 3, 2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc in the same corner. *Ch 1, (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in next corner; repeat from * around. Join. Corners are (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc). Side spaces are ch-1 between groups.
Round 3 Sl st to next corner. In each corner: (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc). Along each side: ch 1, 3 dc in the next ch-1 space, ch 1. Join.
Round 4 (and beyond) Same idea: corners always (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc). Every side ch-1 space gets 3 dc. Put ch 1 between groups. Each new round adds one more 3-dc group per side.
Color changes: Finish the last yarn-over of the join with the new color. Weave tails as you go — future-you will thank present-you.

Checkpoints

  • After Round 1: 4 corners, equal-looking groups.
  • After Round 2: square still sits flat (not cupping or ruffling).
  • Every corner is still (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc).

Troubleshooting

My square cups (bowl shape)

Too few stitches or tight tension. Go up a hook size, or make sure you’re not skipping the side spaces.

My square ruffles

Too many stitches or loose tension. Check you only put one 3-dc group in each side space, not two.

Corners look messy

Always place the corner cluster in the ch-2 space, not in a stitch next to it.

Finishing & joining

Weave in ends on the wrong side. For a blanket, join squares with slip stitch, single crochet, or a flat mattress-style seam. Block lightly if your cotton needs it — steam or wet-block, never high heat on acrylic.

Once this square feels easy, try a solid granny (dc in every stitch with ch-2 corners only) or a two-round mini for bag charms. You’ve got this.

Final note from Sara

Nobody’s first granny is gallery-perfect. Count, breathe, frog if you need to, and make another. The rhythm is the point.
