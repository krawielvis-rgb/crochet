How to crochet a classic granny square

A square that is never just a square: learn the cheerful motif that can become almost anything.

Words by Crochet Journal • Updated September 8, 2026

The granny square is one of crochet’s most adaptable building blocks. Its repeating clusters are rhythmic, portable, and just as satisfying in one color as they are in a joyful mix.

What you will need

Gather a 5 mm hook, worsted-weight yarn, scissors, and a tapestry needle. For a classic look, use three or four colors that have a clear difference in value.

Round one: make a centre

Make a slip knot and chain four. Join the first and last chain with a slip stitch to form a ring. Chain three (this counts as your first double crochet), then work two double crochets into the ring. Chain two. Repeat this cluster-and-corner sequence three more times, then join the round.

Keep building the corners

For each new round, work three double crochets in every open space along the side. In each corner, work three double crochets, chain two, then three more double crochets. The chain-two spaces are what keep your square square.

Count your corners, not every stitch. Four open corners mean you are on track.

Where it can go

Make one as a coaster, join nine into a cushion front, or keep going for a blanket. The humble granny square is an invitation to play with color at your own pace.
